## M-FLOW-087: Receiver-side enforcement is the only working locus for protocol-compliance gates that the sender reliably skips

improve-planning iter-9 P3 and iter-10 P2 attempted to enforce the "first-sentence imperative `Write|Update <path> <verb>`" delegation-prompt protocol via sender-side (orchestrator) textual mitigations: iter-9 P3 added the rule paragraph to `.opencode/agents/devbot.md`; iter-10 P2 added a `pre-send self-check` clause requiring the orchestrator to literally re-read its drafted first sentence and verify form before sending, plus an audit-log `**Prompt opener**:` field to make the check visible. Both mitigations failed identically: iter-10 audit found zero `Prompt opener:` lines in `interactions.md` and stalls doubled (2 → 4) at iter-11. The orchestrator demonstrably knows the rule (correct first-sentence form appears in retry prompts after a stall) but does not consult it at first-draft time. iter-11 P1 shifted the locus: added `## Prompt-opener gate (MUST)` to `src/agents/{po,architect,critic}.md`, requiring the receiving subagent to refuse a delegation lacking the canonical opener with `[BLOCKED] prompt_opener_missing — first sentence was: "..."`, with a research-only exemption for observation imperatives (`Read|Inspect|Report|Analyse`). Pattern precedent: the `@tester` verifying-tool-call gate has held since iter-8 because @tester refuses to accept a developer's "tests pass" claim without independent tool-call evidence — that's a receiver-side gate against a sender-side narration risk.
When a "sender MUST X before Y" rule has empirical evidence that the sender knows X (rule-text present in agent file, correct form appears in retries, knowledge confirmable by inspection) but skips X at draft time, sender-side textual extensions (longer prose, pre-send self-checks, audit-log fields) hit a ceiling: they fail identically to the original rule because the failure mode is non-consultation, not non-knowledge. The structural fix is to reformulate as "receiver MUST refuse Y if X-evidence is absent" — the receiver has structural incentive to comply because a malformed input produces unbounded work otherwise. Diagnostic signal: two consecutive same-locus textual mitigations of the same defect failing identically. Generalises to: any protocol-compliance gate where the sender's incentive to skip (token saving, hurry) outweighs the cost of the rule, and the receiver bears the downstream cost. Pre-emptive heuristic at SKILL-design time: if the rule's beneficiary is the receiver (or downstream artefacts), default to receiver-side enforcement from the outset rather than relying on sender discipline.
